Demand for activity recommendation is large and clearly real. But the head terms are dominated by established incumbents, so a new entrant should target long-tail, seasonal, and location-specific variations rather than the head terms.

## 2. Methodology

- **Sources used** (all free, no account needed):
  1. **seoluma.com** — keyword research tool serving monthly search volume from Google's own keyword data (Google Keyword Planner figures), stated expressly as "Real monthly volumes from Google data. Not third-party estimates." Sourced 2026-10-21.
  2. **KeySearch** — "Top Entertainment Keywords" page reporting global monthly Google search volume. Sourced 2026-10-21.
  3. **SEOpital** — "The Best Entertainment SEO Keywords" page, global monthly volume. Sourced 2026-10-21.
  4. **Venuera** — "What 'Events Near Me' Searches Reveal in 2026", using DataForSEO Labs keyword data (US, English, retrieved 26 July 2026). Sourced 2026-10-21.
  5. **Ryan Robinson / ryrob.com** — free keyword tool, used as an example of seasonal volume spikes. Sourced 2026-10-21.
- **Volume convention**: all figures are *average monthly searches* (12-month average), not a live daily count, and count searches rather than unique people. Statistical figures are estimates and move month to month.
- **Geography**: Where a source provided US figures (seoluma, Venuera/DataForSEO), they are labelled "US". Where only global figures were available (KeySearch, SEOpital), they are labelled "global".
- **Related-terms sourcing**: For some queries (e.g. "attractions and activities near me", "tourist things near me"), the volume page I cited for the head term also lists related terms with volumes on the same page. Those are reported as sourced, with the parent URL noted.

## 3. Limitations & unverified figures (must read)

1. **Exact volume is unavailable for several queries.** "activity ideas", "what should we do", "what should I do", "what to do", and seasonal month-terms ("things to do in January", etc.) returned no data in the sampled free tool. This is a *sampling gap*, not proof of zero demand — these are umbrella/typographic variants that aggregators may not index. Flag as `[unverified]` / "not retrievable".
2. **Tools disagree by ~2–5x on the same term.** Ahrefs notes different tools calculate keyword demand differently and numbers can vary; e.g. "things to do this weekend" is 90,500 (KeySearch) vs 110,000 (SEOpital). Treat any single number as an estimate.
3. **All figures are 12-month averages.** A term that triples in December looks flat in the average. Seasonality can only be seen in the 12-month trend, which free public tools do not expose in bulk — this is why seasonal-month terms are in the "unavailable" section rather than fabricated.
4. **"Near me" volume is a demand pool, not a clean list.** "things to do near me", "events near me", "attractions near me" overlap heavily; one person searching several times is counted multiple times. The 1.68M "events near me" figure is a *sum of 23 distinct queries*, not deduplicated unique searchers.
5. **Google Trends relative volume was not extracted programmatically.** The public Trends API requires application; the explore endpoint returned HTTP 400 from this session, and the chart data is embedded in client-side JS. I therefore did not fabricate a 0–100 relative series. So: relative seasonality (e.g. weekend lifting) is *inferred* from the near-term/seasonal query volumes above, not measured.
6. **Seoluma duplicate/typo entries.** "things to do do near me", "things to do things to do near me", etc. all resolve to the same 2,240,000 figure and appear to be search-quality glitches on the source site. They are excluded from the table to avoid double counting.

## 4. What this means for ThingzToDo (demand read)

- **Demand is large.** Head terms "things to do" + "things to do near me" ≈ **5.0M US searches/month**; the near-me discovery pool (things to do near me 2.24M + events near me 1.68M) clears **~3.9M+ US searches/month**.
- **Demand is real and frequent**, supported by: (a) US head-term volumes in the millions, (b) a 1.68M/month event-discovery pool drawn from 23 distinct queries, (c) near-term "tonight/weekend" intent (33,100 + 33,100 + 1,000 + 210 = ~67,400 immediate events queries US/month), and (d) documented seasonal spikes (board games for kids 1,300→20,000+ at Christmas; outdoors eating 40,500 US/mo).
- **The wedge is not the head terms.** seoluma repeatedly flags "things to do near me" as "a competitive head term dominated by established sites." A new entrant wins on (a) location-specific long-tail, (b) seasonal/weather-triggered queries, and (c) immediate "tonight/weekend" intent. This is the core positioning rationale.
- **Strongest counter-evidence against "a dedicated app will win on volume":** Google, plus Maps, Search AI Overviews, TikTok, and review platforms already capture a large share of "what should we do" intent. High search volume does not equal an unmet problem — it may be well-served by search result pages. The thingz-to-do value is *personalisation by weather/cost/people*, not raw discovery.
